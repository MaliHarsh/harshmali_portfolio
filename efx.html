02 / Ownership

My contribution.

As manufacturing lead for last year’s EFx team, I led manufacturing and assembly of our remotely operated waste-collection robot and built its drivetrain. I contributed to design, fabrication, and testing while working within competition size, safety, and performance constraints. It was my first major solo engineering responsibility within a team, and it required me to connect a subsystem’s design to the practical needs of the full robot.

The CAD and hardware images show the relationship between the intended assembly and the final system. My contribution centered on manufacturing and the drivetrain; the complete robot reflects the team’s work.

Integration beyond the parts list

This architecture explains why a mechanical decision can become an electronics decision: actuator mounting affects cable routing, motor loads affect driver selection, and mechanisms need enough clearance for wires and connections as well as moving parts.

The electronics form a team-level architecture. My manufacturing and drivetrain responsibilities connect to it through component mounting, packaging, and mechanical integration.

Driver details and corrections to the working notes

DRV8825 current regulation depends on the board’s sense resistance: Ilimit = VREF / (5Rsense). A 0.8 A setting corresponds to 0.4 V only with 0.10 Ω sense resistors; it is not obtained by dividing current by the motor winding resistance.

STEP and DIR set motion; mode inputs select microstepping. nRESET and nSLEEP must be high for operation. nENBL disables the driver outputs when high. nFAULT reports a driver fault; it does not independently shut down every motor on the robot.

Direction depends on wiring and reference orientation. A driver-enable signal also does not, by itself, establish a complete emergency-stop system.

Reference: Texas Instruments DRV8825 datasheet ↗. Carrier-board pin numbering must be checked against the actual board.

03 / Reflection

What I gained.

Subsystem ownership

I learned what it means to be responsible for a component that other people depend on. Completing a drivetrain also means thinking about how it interfaces with the frame, power, and the rest of the team’s design.

Manufacturing judgment

The build strengthened my ability to connect CAD geometry with assembly and fit. A model is a starting point; physical integration is where the assumptions become visible.

Confidence through responsibility

Taking on the drivetrain independently gave me a clearer understanding of my own technical gaps and the confidence to work through them. The most valuable outcome was how much more I understood after building it.
