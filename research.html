01 / Research question

What happens inside the process?

Laser powder bed fusion builds a metal part layer by layer. A thin layer of powder is spread across a build surface, and a laser melts selected regions to form the next cross-section. Multi-material processing brings different alloys into this process to combine their properties within a component.

In multi-material laser powder bed fusion, melt-pool behavior and ejected material can influence how two materials mix. Our research studies Cu10Sn bronze and 316L stainless steel using in situ synchrotron X-ray imaging and CFD simulations. My work focuses on the image-processing and analysis side of that research.

The images make fast, small-scale events observable. The analysis challenge is to identify useful features consistently and connect their appearance across frames to the underlying physical process.

04 / Engineering judgment

A measurement needs context.

Scale calibration

Pixel dimensions become physical dimensions only after applying the image calibration. A visible scale bar makes the result easier to interpret and provides a check on presentation consistency.

Time and feature identity

To estimate velocity, a feature must be matched across frames and its displacement divided by the verified elapsed time. Calibration of the time base connects frame-to-frame displacement to physical velocity.

Segmentation quality

A mask simplifies an image, but it also introduces decisions at the boundary. Feature overlap, noise, and changing geometry can affect the measured dimensions, so visual review remains part of the analysis.

05 / Growth

What the research taught me.

I gained experience building repeatable image-processing workflows and presenting outputs that another researcher can interpret. Scale bars, timestamps, clean backgrounds, and structured exports are small implementation details with a large effect on usability.

The work also connected programming to manufacturing physics: the features I measured were evidence of spatter formation, melt-pool instability, and interfacial mixing—not simply shapes in an image.

Expanding into thesis research

I am beginning thesis research that integrates Python and CAD model analysis to characterize and predict the geometry of 3D-printed sand-casting molds. This extends the same interest in turning complex geometry into useful engineering information.
